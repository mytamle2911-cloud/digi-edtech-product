# Product discovery & user needs

## The opportunity
The DIGI proposal identifies an uneven distribution of practical digital-skills learning opportunities, particularly between students with different educational resources.

## User and customer distinction
| Segment | Jobs to be done | Need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secondary/high-school students | Learn how to use AI/digital concepts in real situations | Engaging, bite-sized learning |
| Parents | Support their children's future-ready skills | Affordability, trust, visible learning progress |
| Schools (potential future segment) | Supplement digital literacy | Adaptable learning materials and rollout support |

## Initial product hypothesis
Gamified, mobile-first learning can lower the barrier to practicing AI skills, while explanations of real-world benefits can make the experience more compelling to parents.

## Evidence used
- Comparison of learning programs and digital-skills curriculum.
- Interviews and feedback activities described in the DIGI proposal.
- Competitor and market review.

## Research limitations
The research is directional. User interview counts, collection instruments, and sample composition should be disclosed once verified. Market projections should not be presented as measured user outcomes.
